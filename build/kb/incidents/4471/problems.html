The three problems

Month and day swapped in the shared parser. The parser reads every date month first. A tenant that writes the day first gets a flipped date, and its people see the booking on the wrong day. Tenants on booking-v2, arriving by app or by email.

The booking form prints the wrong order for the tenant. booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY. A day-first tenant reads that hint and types the month first, as it was told to, and the form reads the date the tenant’s way. Same symptom, different cause, different fix: the form and the parser disagree about the same field.

A one-day move, not a swap. These reports were read by the desk as a one-day move, and filed under the parser’s cause because the subject matched. Their dates shift by one day, across a timezone or a daylight-saving change. Month and day are not swapped here, so the parser’s fix will not close them.

What the desk should do

  • Split #4471 into three tickets, one per problem, each with the count in the table above.
  • Fix the parser to read the tenant’s date order instead of month first.
  • Fix booking-v3: it prints MM/DD/YYYY to tenants who write the day first.
  • Move the one-day-move reports out of the parser’s ticket, and give them their own.
  • Record a new decision. The merge rule in decision 0001 merges on the subject line, and a subject line cannot tell a swapped date from a shifted one. A decision is never edited after it is accepted, so this one is replaced, not corrected.
Do not count by intake's tag, and do not count rows. The export repeats reports, and the tag is wrong often enough to change the split.

Lessons

  • One ticket can hold several bugs. Merge early, count once, but split by cause.
  • A duplicate is one id. The export gives the same report more than once.
  • A tag from intake is a guess. Read the tenant, and read the form the tenant runs.
